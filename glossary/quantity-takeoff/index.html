Quantity takeoff

Measuring a design’s drawings to find how much of each material and piece of work the project needs.

Takeoff turns drawings into numbers: square metres of floor, metres of wall, the number of doors, cubic metres of concrete. Those numbers become the bill of quantities, and the bill is what gets priced, ordered and paid for.

Because every later decision rests on these numbers, a good takeoff is one someone else can check: each quantity should point back to the sheet, the scale and the shape it came from.

Worked example

A room measured at 7.00 × 8.00 m gives 56.00 m² of floor screed, 30.00 m of skirting before deducting door widths, and one line in the bill for each.

In QtoMate

Each measurement you draw becomes a line in the bill, with its sheet and scale attached, and you approve lines as you check them.

Questions

Is takeoff the same as estimating?

No. Takeoff finds the quantities; estimating puts prices, labour and overheads against them. Takeoff comes first.

Can takeoff be done from a PDF?

Yes, as long as the scale is known. A PDF exported from CAD also keeps the exact lines, which makes precise measuring possible.

Related terms

  • Bill of quantitiesThe list of every item of work and material in a project, each with a unit and a quantity.
  • Drawing scaleThe ratio between a length on the sheet and the same length in reality, such as 1:100.
  • SnappingMaking the cursor land on an exact point of the drawing, such as a line end or a crossing, instead of wherever you clicked.
