Scale calibration

Setting the scale by measuring a distance whose real length you already know.

You click the two ends of a known dimension and type its real length. The tool divides one by the other and from then on every measurement uses that ratio.

Pick the longest reliable dimension you can find: an error of one click matters less on 12 m than on 1 m. The most common mistake is the unit: typing 6000 when the field expects metres gives a scale a thousand times off.

Worked example

Click the ends of the 12000 mm overall dimension, enter 12000 with the unit set to mm: the result is 1:100. Entering 12000 with the unit set to m would give 1:100000.

In QtoMate

The two clicks snap to the dimension’s ticks, the unit can be mm, cm or m, and a result outside the usual range of drawing scales raises a warning.

Questions

Which dimension should I use?

A long one printed as a number: an overall size or an axis spacing. Avoid dimensions marked “not to scale”.

Do I need to calibrate if the scale is printed?

Not always, but measuring one known dimension after setting the scale is a quick way to catch a resized PDF.

Related terms

  • Drawing scaleThe ratio between a length on the sheet and the same length in reality, such as 1:100.
  • SnappingMaking the cursor land on an exact point of the drawing, such as a line end or a crossing, instead of wherever you clicked.
  • Linear takeoffMeasuring items priced by length: walls, skirting, cable, pipe, kerbs, handrails.
