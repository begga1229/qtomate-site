Bill of quantities

The list of every item of work and material in a project, each with a unit and a quantity.

Each line describes one thing, gives its unit (m, m², m³, no.) and states how much of it there is. Lines are usually grouped by trade or by part of the building so totals can be read at a glance.

Contractors price the bill line by line, and later the same lines are used to measure work done. That is why each quantity should be traceable to where it was measured.

Worked example

Group “Floors”: floor screed 56.00 m², floor screed 17.50 m², opening −4.00 m², subtotal 69.50 m². With a 0.20 m thickness the same lines give 13.90 m³.

In QtoMate

The bill builds itself as you measure. Lines can be grouped, folded, approved and exported to Excel with a summary sheet of totals.

Questions

What is the difference between a BOQ and an estimate?

The bill lists quantities. The estimate multiplies them by rates and adds the other costs.

Should each line have a source?

Yes. A sheet number and a measured shape let anyone check or update the line when the drawing changes.

Related terms

  • Quantity takeoffMeasuring a design’s drawings to find how much of each material and piece of work the project needs.
  • Deductions for openingsSubtracting the parts of a surface that will not be built or finished, such as windows in a wall or a shaft in a slab.
  • Waste factorAn allowance added to a measured quantity for cutting, breakage and offcuts, usually as a percentage.
