Waste factor

An allowance added to a measured quantity for cutting, breakage and offcuts, usually as a percentage.

The bill should show the net quantity as measured. Waste belongs to ordering and pricing: you buy more tiles than the floor area because some are cut and some break.

Typical allowances depend on the material and the layout: a few percent for concrete, more for tiles laid diagonally. Use your own figures from past jobs rather than a general rule.

Worked example

69.50 m² of floor tiles with a 10% allowance: 69.50 × 1.10 = 76.45 m² to order.

In QtoMate

QtoMate keeps the bill net and does not add waste. Apply your allowance in the Excel file, where it is visible to whoever checks the order.

Questions

Why not add waste to the measured line?

Because then nobody can check the measurement against the drawing. Keep the two numbers apart.

Is waste the same as a contingency?

No. Waste covers material you will physically lose; contingency covers risk and is priced separately.
