Drawing scale

The ratio between a length on the sheet and the same length in reality, such as 1:100.

At 1:100, one millimetre on paper is 100 millimetres on site, so 60 mm on the sheet is 6 m. The scale is printed in the title block and sometimes next to each view, because one sheet can hold views at different scales.

A PDF keeps the scale only if it was printed at the right paper size. Resized or “fit to page” prints change it, which is why a quick check against a known dimension is worth doing on every sheet.

Worked example

On an A0 sheet at 1:100, an axis spacing drawn as 170.08 points (60 mm) measures 6.00 m.

In QtoMate

Pick a preset or type a ratio. The scale can apply to every page or only to the current one, and each line in the bill records the scale it was measured at.

Questions

What if the sheet has no scale?

Calibrate from a dimension you trust, such as an axis spacing or an overall size.

Can one sheet have two scales?

Yes, a plan and a detail often share a sheet. Measure each view with its own scale.

Related terms
