Snapping

Making the cursor land on an exact point of the drawing, such as a line end or a crossing, instead of wherever you clicked.

A click is never perfectly accurate. On a sheet viewed at 20%, one screen pixel can be about 18 cm on site. Snapping removes that error by moving the point to the nearest real feature of the drawing within a few pixels.

It needs a vector drawing, where the lines are stored as geometry. In a scanned drawing the lines are only pixels and there is nothing exact to snap to.

Worked example

On the sample sheet, the 12000 mm overall dimension was measured with both clicks up to 6 pixels off target. With snapping, both points landed on the dimension’s ends and the result was exactly 12.00 m.

In QtoMate

Snaps to line ends, midpoints, crossings, dimension ticks and points of your own measurements. A marker shows the type. Press S to switch it off, or hold Alt for one click.

Questions

Why does it sometimes catch the wrong line?

Where many lines meet, zoom in a little or hide the layers you do not need. Hidden layers are never snapped to.

Does it work on scanned drawings?

No. Scans have no vector lines. Measure them by hand after calibrating.

Related terms

  • Drawing layersNamed sets of lines in a drawing, such as walls, dimensions, text or one engineering system, that can be shown or hidden together.
  • Scale calibrationSetting the scale by measuring a distance whose real length you already know.
