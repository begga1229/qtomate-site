Takeoff glossary

The words estimators use every day, explained plainly, each with a worked example.

  • Area takeoff

    Measuring surfaces priced per square metre: floors, ceilings, roofs, slabs and finishes.

    Read →

  • Bill of quantities

    The list of every item of work and material in a project, each with a unit and a quantity.

    Read →

  • Count takeoff

    Counting items priced per piece: doors, windows, sockets, detectors, fittings.

    Read →

  • Deductions for openings

    Subtracting the parts of a surface that will not be built or finished, such as windows in a wall or a shaft in a slab.

    Read →

  • Drawing layers

    Named sets of lines in a drawing, such as walls, dimensions, text or one engineering system, that can be shown or hidden together.

    Read →

  • Drawing scale

    The ratio between a length on the sheet and the same length in reality, such as 1:100.

    Read →

  • Linear takeoff

    Measuring items priced by length: walls, skirting, cable, pipe, kerbs, handrails.

    Read →

  • Quantity takeoff

    Measuring a design’s drawings to find how much of each material and piece of work the project needs.

    Read →

  • Scale calibration

    Setting the scale by measuring a distance whose real length you already know.

    Read →

  • Snapping

    Making the cursor land on an exact point of the drawing, such as a line end or a crossing, instead of wherever you clicked.

    Read →

  • Volume from area and depth

    Getting cubic metres by multiplying a measured area by a thickness or depth.

    Read →

  • Waste factor

    An allowance added to a measured quantity for cutting, breakage and offcuts, usually as a percentage.

    Read →
