Volume from area and depth

Getting cubic metres by multiplying a measured area by a thickness or depth.

Slabs, screeds, insulation and excavation are drawn in plan but priced by volume. Measure the area once, give it a thickness, and the volume follows. If the thickness changes, nothing needs measuring again.

The same idea works for walls: a length times a height gives the wall area for plaster, paint or tiles.

Worked example

A 56.00 m² slab, 0.20 m thick, needs 11.20 m³ of concrete before any allowance for waste.

In QtoMate

Select an area line and fill in “Depth or thickness”. The volume appears on the line, in the group subtotal and in the Excel summary.

Questions

What about slabs with varying thickness?

Split the slab into areas of equal thickness and give each its own value.

Are beams and drops included?

Only if you measure them. Add them as their own lines so they can be checked.

Related terms

  • Area takeoffMeasuring surfaces priced per square metre: floors, ceilings, roofs, slabs and finishes.
  • Deductions for openingsSubtracting the parts of a surface that will not be built or finished, such as windows in a wall or a shaft in a slab.
  • Waste factorAn allowance added to a measured quantity for cutting, breakage and offcuts, usually as a percentage.
